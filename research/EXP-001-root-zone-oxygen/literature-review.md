# Literature Review — EXP-001

**Project:** AeroPod Root-Zone Oxygen and Temperature Optimisation
**Status:** Preliminary literature review — ongoing
**Last updated:** 18 October 2026

## 1. Research Focus
This review examines dissolved oxygen (DO), nutrient solution temperature, and lettuce growth in hydroponic systems, with emphasis on NFT.

## 2. Existing Research

### Goto et al. (1996)
Study: Effect of dissolved oxygen concentration on lettuce growth in floating hydroponics.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.1996.440.36

Lettuce was grown at DO concentrations of 2.1, 4.2, 8.4, and 16.8 mg/L. The authors reported no significant differences in fresh weight or shoot/root dry weight between these treatments.

Relevance: More DO is not automatically associated with greater lettuce growth. This was a floating hydroponic system, not NFT.

### Active aeration study (2026)
Study: Active aeration enhances tissue hydration and fresh mass in hydroponic lettuce and modulates calcium-mobilizing biostimulant efficacy.
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13195060/

In deep-water culture, raising DO from approximately 2.9 to 3.5–4.0 mg/L increased shoot fresh mass at 21 days after transplanting, but did not increase dry mass.

Relevance: Fresh weight and dry weight must be reported separately. Results may not transfer directly to NFT.

### Raising root zone temperature (2024)
Study: Raising root zone temperature improves plant productivity and metabolites in hydroponic lettuce production.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1352331

This NFT study investigated nutrient solution heating under different air-temperature conditions.

Relevance: Root-zone temperature effects depend on the surrounding environment; cooler water is not universally better.

### Root-zone temperature regulation (2025)
Study: The Effects of Root-Zone Temperature Regulation on the Growth and Quality of Hydroponic Lettuce in Summer.
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/15/12/2768

The study tested different root-zone cooling treatments under summer conditions.

Relevance: Provides a methodological reference for temperature control experiments.

## 3. AeroPod Baseline

- NFT cultivation
- Lettuce harvest approximately 26–35 days after transplanting
- LED photoperiod: 16 hours/day
- Nutrient solution temperature: 24–28°C
- DO, PPFD and harvest weight: baseline measurements pending

## 4. Potential Research Gaps

The following are candidate gaps, not yet confirmed as novel:

1. The effect of combined aeration and nutrient temperature management in AeroPod's specific NFT geometry.
2. Growth response under indoor tropical operating conditions.
3. The difference between increased fresh weight and increased dry biomass.
4. Crop productivity relative to added aeration and cooling energy.
5. Low-cost sensor monitoring and repeatable root-zone control.

A broader literature search is needed before making a claim of scientific novelty.

## 5. Working Research Question

Can dissolved oxygen and nutrient solution temperature management increase marketable lettuce productivity in AeroPod NFT hydroponics without disproportionate energy costs?
